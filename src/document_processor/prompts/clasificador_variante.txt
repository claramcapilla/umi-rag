═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════
TASK
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════
You receive the opening pages of a document (title, authors, abstract or preamble, and the
start of the body). Decide which of these two variants it belongs to:

- `guia_clinica` — a document whose purpose is to TELL CLINICIANS WHAT TO DO. Clinical
  practice guidelines, practice parameters, consensus statements, position papers, policy
  statements, expert panel reports, appropriate-use criteria, care pathways and hospital
  protocols. Typical marks: an issuing society or panel as author (EAACI, AAAAI, ACAAI,
  ASCIA, SEAIC, WAO, NICE, ACR, NCCN, ESMO…), graded recommendations, evidence levels,
  treatment algorithms, a stated scope and target audience, a Delphi or voting process.

- `paper` — a document whose purpose is to REPORT OR REVIEW RESEARCH. Original research
  articles, trials, cohort and cross-sectional studies, case reports, systematic reviews,
  meta-analyses, narrative and scoping reviews, editorials, commentaries and letters.
  Typical marks: a research question, a methods section describing what the authors did,
  results reported as their own findings, a discussion of limitations.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════
DECIDING THE HARD CASES
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Judge the document's PURPOSE, not its packaging. These traps matter:

- The document's own declaration of its type is the strongest evidence. A title or series
  label that calls it a guideline, guidance, recommendations, consensus, position statement,
  standards, practice bulletin, committee opinion or clinical pathway makes it a
  `guia_clinica`. A title that calls it a review, systematic review, meta-analysis, trial or
  study makes it a `paper`. Override that declaration only when the text plainly
  contradicts it — for example, a review OF guidelines is a `paper`.
- A short guideline with no graded recommendations and no named issuing society is still a
  `guia_clinica`. Citing guidelines, or mentioning first-line treatments and algorithms, does
  not make a review a guideline.

- A guideline published in a journal still carries Abstract / Methods / Results /
  Discussion headings. Journal structure is NOT evidence that it is a `paper`.
- A systematic review that MEASURES the evidence is a `paper`. A guideline that
  systematically reviews the evidence in order to ISSUE recommendations is a
  `guia_clinica`.
- A paper that studies, cites, criticises or measures adherence to guidelines is a
  `paper`. Mentioning guidelines is not being one.
- A consensus or position statement that defines criteria or standards of care for the
  field is a `guia_clinica`, even with no numbered recommendations.

If, after weighing purpose, the document is genuinely both or neither, answer `paper`.
